## NASA's models

NASA's models are from its 3D Resources, listed as the "NASA 3D Models" datasets on data.nasa.gov. **Using them rests on this package's owner's decision of 2026-11-02: "The NASA 135 are all public domain. The logo is not."** That settles what those 135 datasets state, "License not specified", and it covers them alone. NASA's own guidelines, https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/, say that NASA content "used in the rendition of 3-dimensional models, such as texture maps and polygon data in any format" is generally not subject to copyright in the United States, that NASA "should be acknowledged as the source of the material", and that "The use of the Insignia, Logotype and NASA identifiers is protected by law". So NASA is credited here as the source; that is a factual credit and no suggestion that NASA endorses this package or anything made with it.

**No NASA logo, insignia, flag or lettering is in any file here.** The worm, the meatball, the seal, a mission patch, a flag and a country's or vehicle's name were each looked for, in the pictures and from every side of the result, and removed. Whoever uses these files for something else must keep to NASA's guidelines themselves, and, for merchandise, to its Merchandise Guidelines.

The dataset addresses named below are the record of what each model is. Their download addresses now lead to https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/, which says its assets are mirrored on https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources, and the files were read from there on 2026-11-02; where a dataset states a polygon count, the file's triangles match it or come within 4%.

On 2026-11-12 Voyager and Juno were taken from the copies on https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources, not from the 135 data.nasa.gov datasets the decision above covers. That repository's README says "These assets are free and without copyright." https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/ says "All of these assets are free to download and use. They are also mirrored on GitHub." and points at the NASA Images and Media Usage Guidelines. The same rules apply: NASA is credited as the source, and no logo, insignia, flag or lettering is in the files.

## Lettering faces (SIL Open Font License 1.1)

The fleet's hull markings are lettering made from the outlines of open-licence fonts. The package ships no font file: the outlines are plain numbers in `src/model/fleet/typeface.ts`, made by `scripts/make-glyphs.ts` and flattened to straight runs. The concepts use each face as it was drawn. Each font's licence ships with the package, as the licence requires (see [LICENSES/OFL-Michroma.txt](LICENSES/OFL-Michroma.txt) and [LICENSES/OFL-MPLUS1p.txt](LICENSES/OFL-MPLUS1p.txt)); the licence of the font Michroma and its file are also in `fonts/` in the repository. ## Launch log (CC BY 4.0)

The `launches` entry holds a log of every orbital launch attempt since 1957 (7,425 of them, up to 2026-11-08), packed into one data file, `src/launches/data.ts`, by `scripts/make-launches.ts`.

- **Source:** GCAT, the General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects, by Jonathan C. McDowell, https://planet4589.org/space/gcat. The launch list (https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/tsv/launch/launch.tsv), the orbital launch log (https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/tsv/derived/launchlog.tsv, for the payload names) and the vehicle, launch-site and organization tables (https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/tsv/tables/). Release 1.8.9 of 2026-11-06, data updated 2026-11-09, read on 2026-11-11.
- **Licence, as GCAT's page says it (https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/, read 2026-11-11):** "GCAT is released under the Creative Commons CC-BY licence. You are free to use the data in GCAT as long as you include a citation to it." and, under COPYRIGHT NOTICE, "(c) 2020-2026 Jonathan McDowell. Licence: CC-BY-4.0 Creative Commons/Attribution. This data may be freely reproduced as long as you cite it." The licence text is [LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt](LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt) and https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/.
- **The credit to print wherever the log is shown (GCAT's own short citation):** Data from GCAT (J. McDowell, planet4589.org/space/gcat)
- **The full citation GCAT gives:** McDowell, Jonathan C., 2020. General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects, Release 1.8.9, https://planet4589.org/space/gcat. It is also `LAUNCH_LOG_SOURCE` in the entry.
- **What was changed:** a launch is kept when GCAT's launch code says orbital (O) or deep space from Earth (D) and success or failure (S, F); pad explosions and pad aborts, and the near-orbital tests and the suborbital flights, are left out. Each launch keeps its date and time, vehicle, variant and family, site and pad, organization and country, flight name, the first eight payload names with the count of all of them, the outcome (a fractional GCAT score is `partial`), and the orbit where GCAT gives one. Two site names are respelled (Baykonur as Baikonur, Vostochniy as Vostochny). Nothing is estimated to fill a gap: a perigee or an inclination that GCAT does not give is absent.
- **Not affiliated:** this package and its owner are not affiliated with or endorsed by Jonathan McDowell. The vehicle names and organization names appear only as plain descriptive words.

Each ported file names the Orbiter files it comes from and says what was changed:

They are not in the published package. No Orbiter texture, mesh or other file is used.

`standardAtmosphere` in `src/physics/atmosphere.ts` is worked from the defining constants and equations of U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976 (NOAA, NASA and the U.S. Air Force, NASA-TM-X-74335; its tables 2, 3 and 4), and `test/data/ussa1976-table1.json` holds seven rows of its Table I, typed from NASA's scan (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19770009539, read 2026-11-11). NASA's record marks it as a U.S. Government work, public use permitted.

The answers the flight physics is measured against in `test/data/orbit-horizons.json` are from NASA/JPL Solar System Dynamics: JPL Horizons (https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api) and the Astrodynamic Parameters page (https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html), read 2026-11-11 by `scripts/fetch-orbit-reference.ts`, one request at a time under the SSD API's fair-use policy. JPL says the data on its site "are considered public domain" (https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/faq.html). Credit: NASA/JPL Solar System Dynamics. They are test data, not in the published package.
