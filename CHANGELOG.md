## [0.29.0] - 2026-11-13

- **Sputnik 1**: `rocket="sputnik-1"`, variant `sputnik-1`. ## [0.28.0] - 2026-11-13

- **Mercury-Redstone names its capsule** : the launch-vehicle article, read 2026-11-13, names what it carries as the Mercury spacecraft. That name is `facts.more.payload` on variant `redstone`, with the source and the date. The capsule drawn is still the Friendship 7 part, and that part's file is unchanged.

A sphere 0.60 m across, mass 406.965 kg, 426 retroreflectors and the launch date 1976-05-04, all from the International Laser Ranging Service's page for the satellite, read on 2026-11-13. Both looks are this package's own sphere. No name or mark is drawn. The maker is NASA, from NASA's mission page, which does not say who built it. Slot: `craft`.

- **Starlink** (MODULES-1238): `rocket="starlink"`, variants `f9-1` and `f9-2` (default), named as the operator's filing of 4 October 2022 names them (IBFS File Nos. SAT-LOA-20200526-00055 and SAT-AMD-20210818-00105, Exhibit B). A `webkit-desk` test project (1280 by 900, no touch) joins the others.

## [0.20.0] - 2026-11-12

- **The air in the `physics` entry** (MODULES-1080, step 4 of 4 of MODULES-511): temperature, pressure, density and the speed of sound with height from layered models (`atmosphereAt`), with Orbiter's 2006 models of the Earth, Mars and Venus as data (`EARTH_ATMOSPHERE`, `MARS_ATMOSPHERE`, `VENUS_ATMOSPHERE`), the 1976 U.S. Standard Atmosphere to 86 km from its defining constants (`standardAtmosphere`), the speed through air that turns with its planet (`airVelocity`), `dynamicPressure`, `machNumber` and the drag (`dragAcceleration`). Ported in part from Orbiter's atmosphere modules, `Vesselbase.cpp`, `Planet.h` and `Vessel.cpp`. Tests hold the three models to Orbiter's own compiled code to 1e-12 and the standard to rows of its printed Table I within its five figures, show where Orbiter's Earth parts from the standard (3 per cent in pressure at 38 km), and drop a body to its terminal speed. The guide's new section in English and Japanese, and a Node example.
- **Attitude and thrusters in the `physics` entry** (MODULES-1079, step 3 of 4 of MODULES-511): a craft's attitude as a quaternion (`rotateVector`, `unrotateVector`, `quatFromAxisAngle`, `turnBy`), Euler's equations (`angularAcceleration`), turning forward in time (`stepSpin`, `spinFor`) and place and attitude moved together so a push along the nose follows it (`stepCraft`), the gravity-gradient torque, thrusters as pushes at points (`thrusterPush`, `torqueLimits`, `fireFor`), and the autopilot's kill-rotation and turn-and-hold modes (`killRotation`, `holdDirection`, with an optional roof direction for the roll). Ported in part from Orbiter (`Rigidbody.cpp`, `BodyIntegrator.cpp`, `Vecmat.cpp` and the navcomp of `Vessel.cpp`), each file naming its source. Tests hold the quaternions and Euler's equation to Orbiter's own compiled code to 1e-12, a free tumble's energy to 5e-14 and angular momentum to 2.5e-7, a spin-up and the axisymmetric wobble to their formulas, kill rotation of an 8 m craft stopping in 8.3 s, and a prograde hold from 90 degrees off settling within 0.3 degrees. The guide's new section in English and Japanese, and a Node example.
- **Burns and transfers in the `physics` entry** (MODULES-1078, step 2 of 4 of MODULES-511): burns all at once (`impulse`) or flown (`flyBurn`), pushing `prograde`, `retrograde`, `normal`, `antinormal`, `radial-out`, `radial-in` or along a fixed vector (`directionOf`), for a time or a change of speed, with gravity pulling and the mass falling at the engine's flow, and stopping, marked `short`, at the dry mass; `flyPlan` flies a list of burns, each centred on its planned time (`burnStart`), with the coasts between. Transfers: `speedChangeFor`, `planeChange`, `biElliptic`, `nodes`, `relativeInclination` and `crossings`, and `stateAtTrueAnomaly` and `timeToTrueAnomaly` for timing a burn. Ported in part from Orbiter (its attitude modes, its plane-alignment and transfer displays, and more of its `Elements` class), each file naming its source. Tests: a Hohmann transfer all at once reaches the far circle to the millimetre; flown with finite burns it ends 1.3 km off with a 3 MN engine and 404 km with 91 kN; a transfer that turns the plane in its second burn ends on the equator's circle; the new `Elements` functions match Orbiter's own to the millimetre. The guide's new sections in English and Japanese, and a Node example.
- **Flight physics, the `physics` entry** (MODULES-511, step 1 of 4): orbits from a position and a speed (`elementsOf`, closed and open, and `stateOf` a time later by Kepler's equation, `eccentricAnomaly`), gravity from the bodies of a scene with a body's J2, J3 and J4 (`gravityAt`), a craft moved forward under any acceleration in fixed steps (`propagate`, `path`, `step`; the classic Runge-Kutta and Yoshida's symplectic methods of second to eighth order), and the rocket equation (`deltaV`, `massAfter`, `burnSeconds`, `massFlow`, `exhaustSpeedOf`, and `thrustAt` for an engine in air). Pure, SI units, no dependency. Ported in part from Orbiter (MIT, Copyright (c) 2000-2026 Martin Schweiger; credited in NOTICE.md and `LICENSES/Orbiter-MIT.txt`), each file naming its source. Tests hold it to Orbiter's own `Elements` class (compiled from Orbiter's source) to the millimetre, and to JPL Horizons: the Moon over 28 days within 0.76 km with the Earth's J2, the ISS over 100 minutes within 0.20 km, the Tesla Roadster over half a year within 4,047 km (the rest is sunlight's push, which Horizons fits). A guide in English and Japanese, and a Node example. Burns and transfers, attitude and thrusters, and the air follow.
- **The Roadster with Starman, as a craft and as Falcon Heavy's payload** (MODULES-975): `rocket="roadster"`, variant `first-generation`, is the car Falcon Heavy's test flight carried on 2018-02-06, roof off and its bonnet bare carbon, painted by the 86 guy's model (CC BY 4.0), 502,492 triangles reduced to 38,517 and 547 KB, its badges and lettering left out and scaled to the first generation's sourced 3,946 mm. - Fixed: the canvas had no size limit but a ratio of 2. It is held to 4096 pixels a side and 6 million pixels in all, so a view as wide as a 5K screen is no longer drawn at 15 million.
- The drawing, with no WebGL, is zoomed and moved by its `viewBox` instead of a CSS scale, so it never grows past its box; it is made again at most 20 times a second, never while out of sight (a hidden tab, scrolled away), and is not rebuilt when only the zoom or the move changes.
- The "Tilt to turn" listener is let go when the 3D view is, and does nothing out of sight; it was added again after each return to the screen.
- `drive()` with the same choice again does nothing.
- The demo's auto-turn stops after a minute and when the view leaves the screen; Play starts it again.
- `pixelRatioFor(width, height, deviceRatio)`, with `MAX_PIXELS`, `MAX_SIDE` and `MAX_RATIO`.
- New browser tests: draws per frame, nothing drawing when still or out of sight, no growth of contexts, buffers, vertex arrays or textures over changes, a lost context, and the canvas within its budget from 1x to 5x on a 5K screen.
